Kigali Genocide Memorial

A place of remembrance, education, and hope for the future

About Kigali genocide memorial center

The Kigali Genocide Memorial is the burial site of more than 250,000 victims of the 1994 Genocide Against the Tutsi. It is one of the most important historical and educational sites in all of Africa.

The memorial was opened in 2004, exactly 10 years after the genocide. It has three permanent exhibitions that explain what happened, why it happened, and how Rwanda has rebuilt itself with extraordinary resilience.

Visiting the memorial is a deeply moving experience. It exists so that the world never forgets — and so that such events are never repeated. The peaceful gardens outside are a place for quiet reflection.

What You Will See Inside

  1. The 1994 Exhibition — A full history of the genocide: the colonial roots, the 100 days, the international response, and the aftermath.
  2. The Children's Memorial Room — Dedicated to the children who were killed. Their photos, names, and favourite things are displayed on the walls.
  3. Genocides in Africa Exhibition — Puts Rwanda in the wider context of other genocides that have happened across Africa.
  4. The Memorial Gardens — Beautiful outdoor gardens where you can walk and reflect in peace.
  5. The Mass Graves — The burial site of over 250,000 victims, marked with respect and dignity.
  6. Documentary Film — A screening room shows documentary films about survivor testimonies.

Visitor Information

  • Location: Gisozi, Kigali — about 5km from the city centre
  • Opening Hours: Monday to Sunday, 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM
  • Entry Fee: Free of charge (donations are welcome)
  • Audio Guide: Available in Kinyarwanda, English, French, and German
  • Photography: Not permitted inside the galleries out of respect
  • Dress Code: Smart, respectful clothing is recommended
  • Children: Parental guidance advised — some content is very emotional

The Three Permanent Exhibitions

Exhibition What It Covers Who Should See It
1994 Genocide Against the Tutsi Full history from colonial period to aftermath and reconciliation All visitors — this is the main exhibition
Children's Memorial Personal stories, photos, and names of children who lost their lives Emotionally intense — prepare yourself
Genocides in Africa History of genocide across Africa — context and comparisons Students, researchers, history lovers
